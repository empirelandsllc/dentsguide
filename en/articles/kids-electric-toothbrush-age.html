What Age Can Kids Use an Electric Toothbrush? A Parent's Guide

Electric toothbrushes can be a game changer for kids who rush through brushing. The vibration does a lot of the work, built-in timers keep them going for the full two minutes, and many kids simply think they are more fun. But when is a child actually ready for one?

The short answer: around age 3

Most kids' electric toothbrushes are designed for children aged three and up. By then most baby teeth are in, and children can usually hold the brush and keep their mouth open long enough to cooperate. Before age three, a soft manual brush is all they need, with a parent doing the brushing.

Even with an electric brush, kids need a parent's help until around age six or seven. The brush does the motion, but an adult still needs to make sure every tooth gets covered.

What to look for in a kids' model

Small brush head and soft bristles. A child's mouth is small and their gums are sensitive. An adult-size head will not reach the back teeth properly.

A two-minute timer. Dentists recommend brushing for two minutes, twice a day. A timer, especially one with lights or music, makes that easy to follow.

Gentle power setting. Kids' models run at lower intensity than adult brushes. If a brush has several modes, start with the softest.

Affordable replacement heads. Brush heads should be replaced roughly every three months. Check that replacements are easy to find before you buy.

Battery or rechargeable. Battery models are cheaper up front. Rechargeable ones cost more but keep consistent power and will not die halfway through brushing.

Making the switch

Let your child pick the color or character. Show them the brush, let them feel the vibration on their hand first, then on their teeth with the power off. Turn it on once they are comfortable. Most kids adapt within a few days.

Hold the brush on each tooth for a couple of seconds rather than scrubbing back and forth. The brush does the work.

How much toothpaste?

For ages three to six, the American Dental Association recommends a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste. Teach your child to spit it out rather than swallow it, and skip the rinse afterward so the fluoride stays on the teeth.

The bottom line

Around age three is a good time to try an electric toothbrush, as long as you pick a model made for kids and keep helping until they can do a thorough job on their own. If your child has sensitive teeth, bleeding gums, or any dental issues, ask your dentist which type of brush is best for them.

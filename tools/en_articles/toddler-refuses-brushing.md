If your toddler clamps their mouth shut, twists away, or runs the moment the toothbrush comes out, you are in very good company. Between ages one and three, brushing turns into a power struggle in a lot of homes. The good news: it is a phase, and a few small changes usually turn it around within a couple of weeks.

## First, how much toothpaste?

The American Dental Association recommends fluoride toothpaste from the first tooth. For children under three, use a smear about the size of a grain of rice. From three to six, a pea-sized amount. The tiny amount is enough to protect the teeth and is safe even if your toddler swallows a little.

## 1. Let them hold a brush too

Give your toddler their own brush while you brush with a second one. Holding something gives them a sense of control, which is exactly what the fight is usually about.

## 2. Try a silicone or finger brush

Some toddlers simply dislike the feel of bristles, especially while molars are coming in. A soft silicone brush or a finger brush is gentler and often accepted when a regular brush is not.

[[amz:silicone toddler toothbrush|Silicone toddler brushes]]

## 3. Change positions

Sitting on the floor with your toddler's head in your lap, facing up, gives you a clear view and a steadier hand. Many parents find this "lap position" ends most of the wriggling.

## 4. Make it a song

Two minutes is a long time for a two-year-old. A short brushing song, or a timer that plays music, gives them a clear end point they can see coming.

[[amz:kids toothbrush timer|Brushing timers for kids]]

## 5. Brush the stuffed animals first

Let them "brush" a favorite toy's teeth, then it is their turn. Toddlers love being the expert.

## 6. Use a mirror

Brushing in front of a mirror turns it into something to watch. Let them see the bubbles and count the teeth together.

## 7. Same time, same order, every day

Toddlers handle routines better than surprises. Brush right after bath and before books, every night, and it stops being a negotiation.

## 8. A flavor they like

Strong mint can feel spicy to small kids. A mild fruit-flavored fluoride toothpaste made for toddlers can make a real difference.

[[amz:toddler fluoride toothpaste mild flavor|Toddler fluoride toothpaste]]

## 9. Keep it short on hard days

On a rough night, a quick, calm brush is better than a long battle. The bedtime brush is the one that matters most, so protect that one.

## When to talk to a dentist

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends a first dental visit by age one. If your child seems to be in pain while brushing, has bleeding gums, or still fights every brush after several weeks, it is worth checking with a pediatric dentist.
